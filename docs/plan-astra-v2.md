**1. Wednesday 10:45–14:00 — lock the direction and reach a playable skeleton**

The Wednesday and Thursday windows contain **18 hours 15 minutes**. Budget all reviews, deployments, playtests, and capture inside those hours. Friday adds polish and rehearsal time, not feature capacity.

**Proposed direction: _Borrowed Dawn_.**  
**Hook: “Steal the sunrise from the machines that hoard it.”**

The world survived a dying sun by sealing its fragments inside great machines. Those machines still defend their reserves while the surrounding settlements go dark. You play a small lantern courier carrying the first stolen ember. Defeating a guardian releases its light into the world.

The player is a dark, tapered lantern body with a pale metal cap, two short legs, an amber core, and a needle-like weapon. The silhouette must work without facial features, cloth simulation, or detailed animation.

Use charcoal blue architecture, pale stone platform edges, amber light for the player and discoveries, and vermilion attack signals distinguished by motion and shape as well as color. Thick platforms, offset pillars, recessed machinery, fog, and restrained bloom establish depth. Keep the playable surface unmistakable and foreground scenery clear of combat.

The broader world is designed now; only the first area ships:

| Area | Theme and procedural identity | Guardian and consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **The Cinder Belfry — built** | A cold industrial shrine: stone blocks, suspended chains, hollow cylinders, one enormous dark bell. | **The Bellkeeper**, a squat furnace under a bell-shaped shell. Releasing its Dawn Core relights the shrine and exposes the route onward. |
| **The Glass Orchard — paper only** | The machines once grew replacement sunlight here. Black branching rods support green and gold crystal prisms. | **The Pruner**, a suspended frame with paired blade arms. Closing blades invite a jump; a raised vertical blade invites a dash past its landing point. Phase two sequences these existing threats. Its reward is an upward dash for later exploration. |
| **The Ash Observatory — paper only** | Broken astronomical rings surround a white light suspended over darkness. | **The Last Astronomer**, an articulated ring guardian protecting the final fragment. Its eventual fight tests aerial positioning; returning the fragments forces a choice between restarting the machines and lighting the settlements. |

**V1 scope: four compact rooms with one loop and one optional branch.**

| Room | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Cinder Landing** | Safe start and checkpoint. Show the Bellkeeper through a sealed gate immediately, establishing a destination. |
| **Chain Gallery** | Short jump-and-dash traversal and two separated enemy encounters. Leads toward the boss and exposes the optional branch. |
| **Wick Cache** | A discoverable side room, signalled by warm light through a gap. One guard protects the Longwick upgrade. |
| **Belfry** | Boss chamber. Its approach opens the sealed shortcut to Cinder Landing before combat begins. |

The loop is Landing → Gallery → Belfry approach → Landing. The Cache branches from the Gallery. Exploration means noticing something desirable, choosing a detour, receiving a useful reward, and recognizing a shortcut back to familiar ground. Avoid invisible walls, blind falls, keys with no visible destination, and mandatory backtracking.

**Launch kit and rewards:**

- Ship movement, jump, a directional melee attack, and a horizontal dash with brief invulnerability. Establish movement and jump first, attack second, dash third; all are present at the skeleton gate.
- Dash is defensive and positional. Attacking requires a separate decision. No stamina, combo tree, healing consumables, wall jump, or parry in the launch commitment.
- **Longwick** automatically equips when collected and visibly extends melee reach. It changes how safely the player can punish an opening. The boss remains beatable without it.
- The boss drops the **Dawn Core**. Collecting it relights the area and opens an overlook within an existing room, revealing the Glass Orchard as a distant tableau. This provides a completed local objective and a promise of more.
- No vendor, currency, equipment menu, or inventory management in v1. Future expansion order: parry, upward dash with the next area, then equipment choice and a vendor.

**Checkpoint and death rules:** touching the checkpoint restores health. Death returns control there within two seconds, at full health. Collected loot and opened shortcuts remain; ordinary enemies and an undefeated boss reset. A defeated boss stays defeated. The opened shortcut puts the player back at the boss entrance within ten seconds. There is no corpse retrieval or resource loss. Progress survives death within the session; reload persistence is outside the baseline.

**Exit criterion:** the deployed side-view skeleton supports start, move, jump, attack, dash, one meaningful enemy encounter, travel between two connected spaces, death, and checkpoint respawn. A reachable boss proxy has one telegraphed attack. Three complete loops finish without a blocker. A fresh viewer describes the opening as a side-view game with three-dimensional depth after three seconds.

**Riskiest assumption:** the reusable arena foundation can support readable platform traversal quickly. Its existing movement, camera, and bot behavior are starting material, not evidence that side-view play works.

**First three concrete tasks:**  
(1) Spend at most twenty minutes recording the pivot, proposed scope, room sketch, and world premise; have the human book both colleague sessions.  
(2) Establish the three-verb traversal-and-combat slice, with a visible player silhouette and readable landing surfaces.  
(3) Add checkpoint recovery and the boss proxy, then inspect actual play before expanding the area.

**Playtests inside the block:** movement and landing check around 12:00; combat and death loop around 13:00; deployed acceptance and the three-second visual check around 13:40. Reserve the final twenty minutes for review, deployment, and evidence.

**2. Wednesday 15:00–20:00 — first fun: learn the boss, discover a reward**

Build the Bellkeeper around **two readable attacks and deliberate recovery windows**:

- **Bell sweep:** the shell tilts and the striking arm draws back before a horizontal swing. Dash through or retreat, then close in during recovery.
- **Furnace stomp:** the body rises and light gathers beneath it before a ground wave. Jump the wave, land, and punish.

At half health, the shell cracks and exposes the furnace. Phase two combines sweep into stomp before a longer opening. The escalation is a changed sequence the player can learn. Avoid extra enemies, screen-filling effects, and a wholesale increase in speed.

Ordinary enemies teach this vocabulary separately: one readable melee guard and one small stomp attacker. Place **three enemies total**, with fights separated. Each should make careless forward movement costly without becoming a lengthy obstacle on repeat visits.

**Exit criterion: two distinct first-fun gates pass on the deployed build.**

**Boss first fun, observed during the colleague’s ten-minute session:**

- The player uses attack, jump, and dash from the game’s own instructions.
- Across their encounters, they deliberately avoid both attack types and punish at least one recovery window.
- After taking damage or dying, they can identify the threat and a different response to try.
- A later attempt shows a successful adaptation, or they win through recognizable counterplay.
- After a death, they voluntarily retry without encouragement.

A win is not required. Before this session, the builder must demonstrate that the boss is beatable both with and without Longwick.

**Exploration first fun:**

- Within the first four minutes, the player identifies the boss as their destination.
- They notice and investigate the Cache cue without a verbal hint.
- They collect Longwick and can describe or demonstrate its effect.
- They open the shortcut and recognize where it returns them.

These observations establish a promising local result, not broad validation.

**Riskiest assumption:** this small moveset creates satisfying learning rather than a repetitive damage exchange.

**First three concrete tasks:**  
(1) Finish and tune the two boss attacks, recovery windows, and phase transition before adding scenery.  
(2) Complete the four-room route, optional Cache reward, and boss shortcut.  
(3) Make one uninterrupted title-to-boss playthrough, fix its largest comprehension problem, and prepare the 19:00 uncoached session.

**Playtests inside the block:** boss-only tests at approximately 16:00 and 17:30; full exploration check at 18:15; colleague at 19:00. Give only “Play from the title screen”; observe first, ask recall questions at the end. Reserve 19:10–19:40 for one focused correction and retest, then capture and deploy.

If the gate fails, preserve the build as a **first-fun candidate, gate failed**, with the exact failed observations.

**Process capture**

Reserve twenty minutes at every build gate for review, human-owned snapshot/tag/deployment actions, and evidence. Preserve the retired skeleton plus four side-view milestones: skeleton, first-fun candidate, feature complete, and final. Never overwrite a milestone to improve its historical result.

For each gate, record the exact URL, timestamp, scope, pass/fail observations, reviewer findings and disposition, one screenshot, a short play clip, and the next unresolved risk. Reconcile the existing retired-skeleton evidence entry, which still records deployment verification as pending. Its recorded URL is [the retired arena skeleton](https://jbotoro.github.io/one-more-run/builds/m1-skeleton/).

Log every human code-edit episode immediately: time, each change, why, whether an agent tried first, and verification. Agent edits are not interventions. Keep an actual zero when there were none.

Bookmark transcript moments as they happen with a short identifier, timestamp, transcript location, the decision and alternatives, the human response, and a later link to the observed outcome. Preserve the original exchange. A proposal without a tested consequence is weaker evidence than a modest decision that visibly improved play.

After each playtest, record the confusing moment or failure, choose one hypothesis, change one or two related tuning values, and replay. All gameplay and feel values remain exposed through the existing tuning workflow.
